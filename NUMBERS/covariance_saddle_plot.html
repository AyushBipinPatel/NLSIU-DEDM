Why E(XY) is not E(X)·E(Y)

Each point is lifted from the flat (x, y) cloud onto the surface z = xy. The amber marker sits at the surface height directly above the centroid, E(X)·E(Y). The crimson marker is the average height of the whole lifted cloud, E(XY). Drag to rotate. Move the slider to change how strongly x and y are related.

the dashed vertical segment is the gap between them, which is the covariance
At correlation zero, the cloud spreads evenly across all four quadrants around the centroid, so the lifts above the surface height and the dips below it cancel out, and the two markers sit almost on top of each other. As correlation grows, the cloud leans into the quadrants where x and y move together, both above their means or both below, and those quadrants sit higher on the saddle. The average height then pulls above the surface height at the centroid, and the visible gap grows. That gap is exactly E(XY) minus E(X)E(Y), the covariance.
